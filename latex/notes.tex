\begin{abstract}
We study how the ferromagnetic quantum many-body scar tower of a spin-$1/2$ chain decays under uniform $\sigma^z$ dephasing of rate $\gamma$, comparing the scar state $\ket{-}^{\otimes L}$ with two non-scar product states, the $x$-N\'eel and $z$-N\'eel states. With exact density-matrix evolution up to $L = 14$ and quantum trajectories up to $L = 20$, the $1/e$ rate of the scar's transverse magnetization becomes $L$-independent for $\gamma \gtrsim 0.05$, and its thermodynamic value $\Ginf$ vanishes as $\gamma \to 0$, roughly as $\gamma^{1/2}$ for $0.02 \le \gamma \le 0.2$. The thermal $x$-N\'eel signal instead decays at the closed-system thermalization rate $\approx 4.8$ for every $\gamma \le 0.1$. At $\gamma \le 0.007$ and fixed $L \le 20$, the scar rate sits at the finite-size cap $\Ge \approx \gamma(L-1)$. A single-kick model, in which every $\sigma^z$ kick damages the coherence as one flip evolved with $H$ does, reproduces the measured rate at every $L$ and $\gamma \le 0.05$ to within $\approx 5\%$, without fit parameters. The thermodynamic lifetime at small $\gamma$ is therefore fixed by how the damage of a single kick grows in time; $L \le 24$ does not yet decide between ballistic ($\Ginf \propto \gamma^{1/2}$) and diffusive ($\propto \gamma^{2/3}$) growth.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model}

\paragraph{Hamiltonian.} Spin-$1/2$ ring of $L$ sites (periodic boundaries), $S^\alpha_j = \sigma^\alpha_j/2$, bond singlet projector $R_{j,j+1} = \tfrac14 - \mathbf S_j\cdot\mathbf S_{j+1}$:
\begin{equation}
H = H_I + h S^z_{\mathrm{tot}}, \qquad
H_I = \sum_j \Big[ \sum_\alpha \big( a_\alpha R_{j,j+1}\sigma^\alpha_{j+2} + b_\alpha \sigma^\alpha_{j-1}R_{j,j+1} \big)
+ \sum_{\alpha\beta} c^{(j)}_{\alpha\beta}\, \sigma^\beta_{j-1} R_{j,j+1} \sigma^\alpha_{j+2} \Big].
\end{equation}
Every term of $H_I$ annihilates the ferromagnetic multiplet $\ket{\Phi_n} \propto (S^-_{\mathrm{tot}})^n\ket{\uparrow\cdots\uparrow}$, $n = 0,\dots,L$, so $H\ket{\Phi_n} = h(L/2-n)\ket{\Phi_n}$: an equally spaced tower of $L+1$ exact scars.

\paragraph{Coefficients.} $h = 1$, $a = b = (1, 1, 1)$, and $c_{xx} = c_{yy} = c_{zz} = 1$ (all other $c_{\alpha\beta} = 0$), each of the three multiplied on every bond $j$ by an independent factor uniform in $[0.5, 1.5]$. The random $c$ removes translation and reflection symmetry; $a$ and $b$ break SU(2), U(1), spin inversion and complex conjugation. One realization per $L$ (seed $1000 + L$), shared by every run. All results in Secs.~\ref{sec:states}--\ref{sec:kick} use these coefficients; the earlier results with different coefficients are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:old}.

\paragraph{Dephasing.}
\begin{equation}
\dot\rho = \Lv\rho = -i[H,\rho] + \gamma\sum_j \big(\sigma^z_j\rho\,\sigma^z_j - \rho\big).
\end{equation}
In the Pauli basis $\rho = 2^{-L}\sum_P r_P P$ the dissipator is diagonal, $\D(P) = -2\gamma\, n_{xy}(P)\, P$, with $n_{xy}(P)$ the number of sites carrying $\sigma^x$ or $\sigma^y$. A single spin's transverse coherence decays as $e^{-2\gamma t}$. The dissipator does not respect the scars.

\paragraph{Methods.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Exact evolution} of the real Pauli-coefficient vector ($\dim = 4^L$, no symmetry is left with random $c$), $\exp(\Lv\,dt)$ by a Taylor series summed to round-off, matrix-free for $L \ge 11$. Up to $L = 14$.
\item \textbf{Diffusive trajectories:} the dephasing as a fluctuating $z$ field, one step
\[ e^{-iH dt/2}\,\prod_j e^{-i\sqrt\gamma\,\Delta W_j\sigma^z_j}\,e^{-iH dt/2}, \]
error of the average $O(dt^2)$; 224 trajectories ($112$ at $L = 20$), $L = 12,\dots,20$.
\item \textbf{Jump trajectories:} since $(\sigma^z_j)^2 = 1$, the effective Hamiltonian is $H$ up to a constant, and a trajectory is exact unitary evolution under $H$ interrupted by $\sigma^z_k$ kicks, with exponential waiting times of total rate $\gamma L$ and a uniform kicked site. The sampling is exact; from the scar state, the state before the first kick is the analytic product state. 448 trajectories (224 at $L = 18, 20$, $\gamma < 0.002$) at the small $\gamma$ of Secs.~\ref{sec:small}--\ref{sec:kick}.
\item \textbf{Single flip:} one kick $\sigma^z_k$ on the noiseless scar state at phase $\varphi = ht'$, then evolution with $H$ only (Sec.~\ref{sec:kick}).
\end{itemize}
Validation: diffusive and jump trajectories agree with the exact $\rho$ at $L = 8$, $\gamma = 0.1$ and $0.01$ (2000 trajectories, rms deviation $\approx 1$ standard error); at the production sizes see Table~\ref{tab:cross}.

\section{How the decay rates are computed}
\label{sec:rates}

All rates in Secs.~\ref{sec:states}--\ref{sec:kick} are obtained the same way, from the time series of one signal $S(t)$ per run: $m_\perp$ for the scar, $s_\perp$ for the $x$-N\'eel state, $\lvert s_z\rvert$ for the $z$-N\'eel state (Table~\ref{tab:states}). All three start at $S(0) = 1$. Code: \texttt{rates} in \texttt{plot\_scripts/fit\_states.py} and \texttt{rate} in \texttt{fit\_small\_gamma.py}.

\paragraph{Envelope.} The signal precesses (the scar's $m_\perp$ is smooth, but $s_z$ crosses zero, and small systems show revivals), so the rate is read from the non-increasing envelope
\begin{equation}
E(t) = \max_{t' \ge t} S(t'),
\end{equation}
computed on the output grid ($dt = 0.1$, or $0.25$ for the small-$\gamma$ jump runs).

\paragraph{The $1/e$ rate $\Ge$ (main rate).} $t_e$ is the first time with $E(t_e) < 1/e$. Between the two output times $t_{i-1}$, $t_i$ that bracket the crossing it is interpolated linearly in $\ln E$:
\begin{equation}
t_e = t_{i-1} + (t_i - t_{i-1})\,\frac{\ln E_{i-1} + 1}{\ln E_{i-1} - \ln E_i}, \qquad \Ge = \frac{1}{t_e}.
\end{equation}
This is \emph{not} a fit of $A_0 e^{-\Gamma t}$: there is no free amplitude and no fit window. It is the rate of the exponential that starts at the true initial value $S(0) = 1$ and passes through the measured envelope at $1/e$. For a single exponential $e^{-\Gamma t}$ it returns $\Gamma$ exactly. For a general decay, writing $E(t) = \exp[-\int_0^t \lambda(t')\,dt']$ with the instantaneous rate $\lambda = -d\ln E/dt$,
\begin{equation}
\Ge = \frac{1}{t_e}\int_0^{t_e} \lambda(t)\,dt,
\end{equation}
the instantaneous rate averaged from $t = 0$ to the $1/e$ time. This matters here because the decays are not single exponentials. The scar decays multi-exponentially, and at small $\gamma$ it follows $\exp[-\gamma\int\Db]$, whose instantaneous rate grows from $2\gamma$ to $\gamma D_{\mathrm{sat}}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:kick}). A fit of $A_0 e^{-\Gamma t}$ with $A_0$ free would instead depend on the window (Appendix~\ref{app:old}): with an early fast drop it gives $A_0 < 1$ and the slower late rate. The single-kick prediction of $\Ge$ uses the same definition, $\gamma\int_0^{t_e}\Db = 1$.

\paragraph{Statistical error (trajectories).} With $\sigma_m(t)$ the standard error of the trajectory-averaged signal (from the errors of $m_x$, $m_y$ or $s_x$, $s_y$), the error of the crossing time is $\sigma_t = \sigma_m(t_e)/\lvert dE/dt\rvert$, with the slope taken over $\pm 4$ output steps, so that $\sigma_\Gamma = \Ge^2\sigma_t$. For an exponential near $t_e$, $\sigma_\Gamma/\Ge \approx e\,\sigma_m$. The exact evolution has no statistical error.

\paragraph{Tail rate $\Gamma_{\mathrm{tail}}$.} A least-squares straight-line fit of $\ln E$ between the times where $E$ drops below $10^{-2}$ and $10^{-3}$. This one \emph{is} an $A_0e^{-\Gamma t}$ fit, with $A_0$ free, on the tail only; it measures the slowest modes (the tower edges for the scar). It is used for exact runs only: trajectory runs reach their statistical floor in that window. Runs stop once the signal stays below $\max(10^{-3}, 3\sigma)$ for 10 time units, or at a fixed $t_{\max}$ (the small-$\gamma$ runs, $t_{\max} = 3/(\gamma(L-1))$, past $t_e$).

\paragraph{Thermodynamic rate $\Ginf$.} For every state and $\gamma$, $\Ge(L) = A + B/L$ is fitted (unweighted) on $L \ge 8$ and $\Ginf = A$. The error is the larger of the fit error and $\lvert A - A_{\mathrm{quad}}\rvert$, with $A_{\mathrm{quad}}$ the intercept of a quadratic fit in $1/L$ on $L \ge 6$. Three groups of sizes and $\gamma$ are handled differently:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{N\'eel states with finite-size revivals.} The affected small sizes are left out: $x$-N\'eel $L = 6$ at $\gamma \le 0.05$, $z$-N\'eel $L = 6, 8$ at $\gamma \le 0.02$. $z$-N\'eel has no quadratic fit; its error is $\lvert A - \Ge(L_{\max})\rvert$.
\item \textbf{Scar at $\gamma < 0.01$.} Every size sits at the cap $\Ge \approx \gamma(L-1)$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:small}), so the $L \ge 8$ fit is not used. Instead:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item $\Ge$ of the largest size is a lower bound on $\Ginf$, since $\Ge$ grows with $L$ there.
  \item A second fit $A + B/L$ on $L = 14$--$20$ only gives an estimate. Its error is the larger of the fit error and the change of $A$ when $L = 12$ is added (Table~\ref{tab:large}).
  \item The collapse $\Ge/\gamma = \xi f(L/\xi)$, $\xi = \gamma^{-\nu}$, gives the power law $\Ginf \propto \gamma^{1-\nu}$.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\gamma = 0$.} It is not fitted. Its $\Ge$ is the mean over the fitted sizes; the scar never reaches $1/e$, so its $\Ge$ is $0$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Exact results}
\label{sec:exact}

These hold for any coefficients.

\paragraph{Dark states and steady states.} $\ket{\Phi_0} = \ket{\uparrow\cdots\uparrow}$ and $\ket{\Phi_L} = \ket{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}$ are eigenstates of $H$ and of every $\sigma^z_j$, hence dark states of the dissipator. The kernel of $\Lv$ is spanned by
\begin{equation}
\mathds{1}, \qquad \dyad{\Phi_0}{\Phi_0}, \qquad \dyad{\Phi_L}{\Phi_L},
\end{equation}
and ED finds exactly three zero eigenvalues for $L = 4, 5, 6$. The infinite-temperature state is therefore \emph{not} the unique steady state. The coherence $\dyad{\Phi_0}{\Phi_L}$ is an eigenmode with $\lambda = -2\gamma L - ihL$, which saturates the general bound $-2\gamma L \le \operatorname{Re}\lambda \le 0$.

\paragraph{Edge coherences of the tower.} Since $\sigma^z_j\ket{\Phi_0} = \ket{\Phi_0}$ and $\sum_j\sigma^z_j = 2S^z_{\mathrm{tot}}$,
\begin{equation}
\D\big(\dyad{\Phi_0}{\Phi_n}\big) = \gamma\,\dyad{\Phi_0}{\Phi_n}\Big(\sum_j\sigma^z_j - L\Big) = -2n\gamma\,\dyad{\Phi_0}{\Phi_n},
\end{equation}
and both states are eigenstates of $H$, so $\dyad{\Phi_0}{\Phi_n}$ (and likewise $\dyad{\Phi_L}{\Phi_{L-n}}$) is an exact Liouvillian eigenmode with
\begin{equation}
\lambda = -2n\gamma - i h n \qquad \text{for every } L.
\end{equation}
The slowest tower modes therefore decay at exactly $2\gamma$, independent of $L$. In first-order perturbation theory the coherences $\dyad{\Phi_n}{\Phi_{n+1}}$ decay at $\gamma L(1 - m_n m_{n+1})$ with $m_n = 1 - 2n/L$: $2\gamma$ at the edges, $\approx\gamma L$ in the middle of the tower. The scar signal is a binomially weighted sum of these, which makes it multi-exponential.

\paragraph{Initial loss of tower weight.} For $\rho = \dyad{\psi}{\psi}$ with $\ket\psi = \ket{-}^{\otimes L}$, $w = \Tr(\Ptow\rho)$ and $[\Ptow,H] = 0$,
\begin{equation}
\dot w(0) = \gamma\sum_j\big(\lVert\Ptow\sigma^z_j\ket\psi\rVert^2 - 1\big), \qquad
\Ptow\sigma^z_j\ket\psi = \tfrac{2}{L}S^z_{\mathrm{tot}}\ket\psi
\;\Rightarrow\; \dot w(0) = -\gamma(L-1),
\label{eq:wdot}
\end{equation}
using permutation symmetry of the tower and $\langle (S^z_{\mathrm{tot}})^2\rangle = L/4$. A kick keeps a fraction $1/L$ of the state in the tower; the rest is $k \ne 0$ spin waves. Reproduced exactly by the numerics for $L = 4,\dots,8$. At late times $w \to (L+1)/2^L$.

\paragraph{Overlaps.} $\Ptow\sigma^\alpha_j\Ptow = \tfrac2L S^\alpha_{\mathrm{tot}}\Ptow$ for every $j$, so the staggered operators $O^\alpha_\pi = \sum_j (-1)^j\sigma^\alpha_j$ have zero tower part. The $x$-N\'eel signal (Sec.~\ref{sec:states}) is therefore blind to the tower.

\subsection{Thermal references}

\paragraph{$x$-N\'eel.} At $\gamma = 0$ it thermalizes with $\Ge \approx 4.8$ for every $L \ge 8$ ($L = 4, 6$ have finite-size revivals and never reach $1/e$ at $\gamma \le 0.05$). $\Ge$ is $L$-independent for $L \ge 8$ at every $\gamma$, within $\approx 1\%$ (the $L = 16$ realization lies $\approx 4\%$ lower at every $\gamma$, also at $\gamma = 0$: a property of its $c$). $\Ginf$ stays at the closed-system rate for $\gamma \le 0.1$ and then approaches $2\gamma$ ($+0.6$ at $\gamma = 10$): there is no Zeno slowdown (Fig.~\ref{fig:neelx}).

\paragraph{$z$-N\'eel.} At $\gamma = 0$, $\lvert s_z\rvert$ decays at $\Ge \approx 5$ for $L \ge 10$ ($L = 4$ has strong revivals). $\Ginf \approx 5.0$ for $\gamma \le 0.05$, then decreases, $\propto 1/\gamma$ at large $\gamma$ ($0.98$ at $\gamma = 10$): a Zeno slowdown, as expected for a signal the bath cannot see directly (Fig.~\ref{fig:neel}). The number of down spins (Fig.~\ref{fig:neeln}) rises at $\gamma = 0$ from $L/2$ to $\approx 0.55L$ ($m_z \approx -0.1$, the thermal value fixed by the energy since $H$ contains $hS^z_{\mathrm{tot}}$), and with dephasing overshoots and relaxes back to $L/2$ at $\approx 1.5$--$2.3\gamma$, $L$-independent for $L \ge 8$.

\paragraph{Scar against thermal.} $\Ginf(\mathrm{scar}) < \Ginf(x\text{-N\'eel})$ at every $\gamma$, by a factor $37$ ($\gamma = 0.01$), $22$ ($0.02$), $14$ ($0.05$), $11$ ($0.1$), $7.7$ ($0.2$), $4.3$ ($0.5$), $2.6$ ($1$), $1.7$ ($2$), $1.05$ ($10$). The scar rate goes to $0$ as $\gamma\to0$; the thermal rate stays at $\approx 4.8$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:ginf}). This is the expected contrast of the Introduction of the project: scar revivals are regular in the weak-dissipation limit, the thermal signal is not. The $z$-N\'eel rate crosses the scar rate at $\gamma \approx 1.5$, an artefact of its Zeno protection.

\section{Small $\gamma$: the finite-size cap}
\label{sec:small}

To reach the regime $\gamma \to 0$, the scar was run at $\gamma = 0.0005$, $0.0007$, $0.001$, $0.0014$, $0.002$, $0.003$, $0.004$, $0.005$, $0.007$ with jump trajectories for $L = 12,\dots,20$ (without $0.0007$, $0.0014$ at $L = 18, 20$) and exactly for $L = 8, 10$ (every $\gamma$) and $12$ ($\gamma \ge 0.002$), up to $t = 3/(\gamma(L-1))$. The traj-seeds of one $(L,\gamma)$ are pooled, weighted by their trajectory counts.

\paragraph{Cross-checks.} The jump trajectories have a large per-trajectory spread ($\approx 0.3$ at $m_\perp \approx 0.5$, against $\approx 0.08$ for diffusive trajectories at $L = 12$): a trajectory is either undamaged or carries a damaged region. With 448 trajectories the statistical error of $\Ge$ is $1.5$--$6\%$, up to $7.5\%$ at $L = 20$ with 224. All $\gamma$ of one $L$ use the same random numbers, so the kick sequences coincide up to the time scale $1/\gamma$ and the errors are correlated across $\gamma$. Table~\ref{tab:cross} compares jump runs with the exact evolution and with diffusive trajectories: the differences are within $\approx 2\sigma$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:cross}); exact and diffusive agree with each other to $0.3\%$.

\paragraph{Collapse.} If the finite-size effects are set by one length $\xi(\gamma) = \gamma^{-\nu}$, then $\Ge/\gamma = \xi f(L/\xi)$ with $f(x) \approx x$ at small $x$ (the cap) and $f \to$ const at large $x$, so that $\Ginf \propto \gamma^{1-\nu}$. Minimizing the spread of $(\Ge/\gamma)\gamma^\nu$ at common $L\gamma^\nu$ ($\gamma \le 0.05$, $L \ge 8$) gives $\nu = 0.33$, with a cost within a factor 2 of the minimum for $\nu = 0.24$--$0.48$ (the cost at $\nu = 0.5$ is $2.2\times$ the minimum). Ballistic spreading of the damage ($\xi \sim vt$, $\nu = 1/2$, lifetime $\propto\gamma^{-1/2}$) and diffusive spreading ($\nu = 1/3$, lifetime $\propto\gamma^{-2/3}$) are both within this range: $L \le 20$ does not separate them.

\paragraph{Extrapolation from the large sizes.} The fit $A + B/L$ on $L \ge 8$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:states}) includes sizes that are still at the cap when $\gamma$ is small. A second fit uses only $L = 14, 16, 18, 20$. Its error is the larger of the fit error and the change of $A$ when $L = 12$ is added. \begin{itemize}
\item At $\gamma \ge 0.05$ the two fits agree.
\item At $\gamma = 0.01$ and $0.02$ they differ by $15\%$ and $8\%$, with opposite signs: the $L \ge 8$ value at $\gamma = 0.01$ ($0.128$) is pulled down by the capped small sizes.

A power law $A \propto \gamma^{p}$ fitted to all ten values ($0.0005 \le \gamma \le 0.05$) gives $p = 0.58 \pm 0.01$ (weighted) or $0.66 \pm 0.04$ (unweighted), i.e.\ $\nu = 1 - p = 0.34$--$0.42$, consistent with the collapse. Restricted to $\gamma \le 0.007$ it gives $p = 0.80 \pm 0.06$. That steepening is expected from the fit itself: at smaller $\gamma$ the sizes are closer to the cap, so the extrapolation underestimates $\Ginf$ more.

\section{Single-kick test}
\label{sec:kick}

\paragraph{Model.} Take a kick $\sigma^z_k$ at time $t'$ on the otherwise noiseless scar state, and evolve it with $H$ for a time $\tau$. With $c_j(\tau) = (\langle\sigma^x_j\rangle + i\langle\sigma^y_j\rangle)$ divided by its noiseless value at $t' + \tau$, define the damage
\begin{equation}
D(\tau) = \sum_j \big[1 - \operatorname{Re} c_j(\tau)\big], \qquad D(0) = 2,
\end{equation}
and its average $\Db(\tau)$ over 16 phases $\varphi = ht'$ and the kicked sites. If the damage of different kicks is independent and multiplies, each kick multiplies $m_\perp$ by $1 - D/L$. With kicks at total rate $\gamma L$, the Poisson average gives
\begin{equation}
m_\perp(t) \approx \exp\Big[-\gamma L \int_0^t \frac{\Db(\tau)}{L}\,d\tau\Big] = \exp\Big[-\gamma\int_0^t \Db(\tau)\,d\tau\Big],
\label{eq:kick}
\end{equation}
which reproduces the free-spin $e^{-2\gamma t}$ for $D \equiv 2$. The predicted $\Ge$ solves $\gamma\int_0^{t_e}\Db = 1$, with no free parameter. Runs: all sites for $L = 12,\dots,20$ up to $\tau = 25$, and four sites ($0, L/4, L/2, 3L/4$) for $L = 22, 24$ up to $\tau = 8$. Beyond $\tau = 25$, $\Db$ is continued with its mean over $20 \le \tau \le 25$.

\paragraph{Damage.} Fig.~\ref{fig:damage}. $\Db$ jumps from $2$ to $\approx 5.9$ within $\tau = 1$ for every $L$, then grows, and saturates with slow oscillations (period $\approx 6$). At $\tau = 4$: $9.10, 9.99, 10.66, 11.02, 11.35, 11.49, 11.60$ for $L = 12, 14, \dots, 24$; at $\tau = 8$: $9.73, 11.02, 12.36, 13.44, 14.62, 15.54, 16.32$. The saturation value is $11.0, 12.4, 14.0, 15.4, 17.1$ for $L = 12,\dots,20$, i.e.\ $\approx L - 1$ at $L = 12$ and still $0.6$--$1.9$ below $L - 1$ at $\tau = 25$ for the larger sizes. The size dependence sets in at $\tau \approx 3$ ($L = 12$) to $\tau \approx 6$ ($L = 22$ against $24$). For $L = 22, 24$, $\Db$ grows by $\approx 1.0$--$1.2$ per unit of $\tau$ between $\tau = 4$ and $8$, roughly linearly (only four kicked sites are sampled; the spread over the 64 runs is $\approx 1.2$ at $\tau = 8$).

\paragraph{Finite $L$.} The prediction~\eqref{eq:kick} follows the whole measured time series (Fig.~\ref{fig:series}). Over all 55 points ($L = 12,\dots,20$, $\gamma \le 0.05$), the ratio of measured to predicted $\Ge$ is $0.90$--$1.19$, mostly $0.95$--$1.05$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:kickrates}, left). At $\gamma < 0.01$ this is within the statistical errors. At $\gamma = 0.01$--$0.05$, where the errors are below $1\%$, the ratio is systematically $0.95$--$1.00$: the independent-kick formula counts overlapping damage twice, so it predicts a slightly faster decay. So at small $\gamma$ the scar decay is fully set by isolated kicks, and the finite-size cap $\Ge/\gamma \to L - 1$ is the saturated damage $D_{\mathrm{sat}} \approx L - 1$.

\paragraph{Thermodynamic limit.} Fig.~\ref{fig:kickrates}, right: $\Ginf$ against the prediction from $\Db$ at $L = 24$ ($\tau \le 8$, so $\gamma \ge 0.02$). The two agree at $\gamma = 0.05$ ($0.332$ against $0.335 \pm 0.014$). Above that, kicks overlap within the damage time, and the prediction is too fast: $0.53$ against $0.45$ at $\gamma = 0.1$, $0.84$ against $0.65$ at $0.2$, $1.54$ against $1.24$ at $0.5$. At $\gamma \ge 2$ both go to $2\gamma$. At $\gamma = 0.02$ the prediction ($0.181$) is $15\%$ below the $L \ge 8$ extrapolation ($0.213 \pm 0.010$), but only $7\%$ below the fit on $L \ge 14$ ($0.196 \pm 0.003$, Table~\ref{tab:large}), and close to the largest measured size ($\Ge(L = 20) = 0.173$). The remaining gap is either $\Db$ still growing beyond $L = 24$ at $\tau \approx 5$, or the extrapolation.

\paragraph{Consequence.} In the thermodynamic limit at small $\gamma$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:kick} makes the lifetime a property of $\Db_\infty(\tau)$ alone. Linear growth $\Db \simeq 2v\tau$ (ballistic spreading of the damage) gives $\gamma v t_e^2 = 1$, i.e.\ $\Ginf = (\gamma v)^{1/2}$. Diffusive growth $\Db \propto \tau^{1/2}$ gives $\Ginf \propto \gamma^{2/3}$. If the damage stays bounded, $\Ginf \propto \gamma$. The measured $\Ginf$ ($\Ginf^2/\gamma \approx 2.0$--$2.3$ for $0.02 \le \gamma \le 0.1$) and the roughly linear growth of $\Db$ at $L = 22, 24$ for $4 \le \tau \le 8$ favour the ballistic case, but the collapse admits both. Deciding needs $\Db$ at longer $\tau$ in larger systems.

\section{Summary and open points}

\begin{itemize}
\item The scar transverse magnetization decays at an $L$-independent rate once $L - 1 \gg \Ginf/\gamma$. Its thermodynamic rate vanishes as $\gamma\to0$, roughly as $\gamma^{1/2}$ for $0.02 \le \gamma \le 0.2$, and tends to $2\gamma$ at large $\gamma$. The thermal $x$-N\'eel signal decays at the closed-system rate $\approx 4.8$ for $\gamma \le 0.1$, so the two differ by a factor $37$ at $\gamma = 0.01$. The $z$-N\'eel state is Zeno-protected and not a fair reference.
\item At fixed $L$ and $\gamma \to 0$, $\Ge/\gamma \to L - 1$, the loss rate of the tower weight: the local signal then behaves like a global one.
\item Extrapolating only the large sizes ($L = 14$--$20$) gives estimates of $\Ginf$ down to $\gamma = 0.0005$ ($\Ginf/\gamma \approx 18$--$34$ for $\gamma < 0.01$, not converged), consistent with $\Ginf \propto \gamma^{0.58}$--$\gamma^{0.66}$ over $0.0005 \le \gamma \le 0.05$ and with the collapse.
\item A single kick evolved with $H$ predicts the scar decay at every $L \le 20$ and $\gamma \le 0.05$ to within $\approx 5\%$ without parameters. The small-$\gamma$ lifetime is therefore the integral of the single-kick damage.
\item Open: the growth law of $\Db_\infty(\tau)$ (ballistic or diffusive) at $\tau > 8$, which fixes the exponent of $\Ginf$ as $\gamma \to 0$; one disorder realization per $L$; the $\gamma = 0.01$--$0.02$ extrapolation, which is the least converged.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Scar versus non-scar probes}

Three probes, evolved with the same Lindbladian (random $c$, $L = 4,\dots,8$, $\gamma = 0$ and $0.01$):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{P1, scar:} $m_\perp(t)$ from $\rho_0 = \dyad{-}{-}^{\otimes L}$.
\item \textbf{P2, energy:} $C(t) = \Tr[\tilde H e^{\Lv^\dagger t}(\tilde H)]/\Tr\tilde H^2$ with $\tilde H = H - hS^z_{\mathrm{tot}}\Ptow = (1-\Ptow)H(1-\Ptow)$, conserved at $\gamma = 0$ and without tower component.

\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Caveats.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The scar decay is multi-exponential (Fig.~\ref{fig:probes}, left): mid-tower coherences ($\sim\gamma L$) dominate early, the exact $2\gamma$ edge modes late. $\Gamma_{\mathrm{scar}}$ and its $L$-exponent depend on the fit window.
\item $L = 4,\dots,8$ is a factor of two in $L$: power law and exponential fit about equally well; the exponents are descriptive.
\item For P3, a window that ends at twice the noise floor lengthens with $L$ and produces a spurious growth of $\Delta\Gamma$ ($1.43, 2.25, 4.39$); only common windows are meaningful.
\item The predicted initial rate of P2, $2\gamma\sum_P n_{xy}h_P^2/\sum_P h_P^2$, is $2.7$--$2.9\,\gamma$; the long-time decay is slower ($1.8$--$2.3\,\gamma$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Liouvillian spectrum}

Full ED at $\gamma = 0.01$ for $L = 4, 5, 6$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:spectrum}). Each right eigenvector $R$ is split into tower ($\lVert\Ptow R\Ptow\rVert$), mixed and bulk weights. Findings:
\begin{itemize}
\item three zero modes (Sec.~\ref{sec:exact}), and $\operatorname{Re}\lambda \ge -2\gamma L$ saturated by $\dyad{\Phi_0}{\Phi_L}$;
\item the bulk is centred at $-\operatorname{Re}\lambda\approx\gamma L$ (the mean dephasing rate of a Pauli string);
\item the slowest tower modes sit at exactly $2\gamma$ (edge coherences); the next ones at $4\gamma$ for $L = 5, 6$ ($2.94\gamma$ at $L = 4$);
\item the slowest bulk mode is at $1.67\gamma$, $1.66\gamma$, $1.88\gamma$ for $L = 4,5,6$, comparable to the P2 rate;
\item modes with tower weight $>0.9$: 22 of 25, 32 of 36, 38 of 49: at $\gamma=0.01$ some tower coherences already hybridize with the bulk.
\end{itemize}
Checks: at $\gamma = 0$ the dense spectrum equals $-i(E_m - E_n)$ to $5\times10^{-14}$ ($L = 4, 5$); $\sum\lambda = \Tr\Lv = -\gamma L4^L$; $L+1$ eigenstates of $H$ in the tower at energies $h(L/2-n)$.

Three regimes for the scar (Fig.~\ref{fig:gamma}): weak dephasing, $\gamma\le0.05$, with $\Gamma\propto\gamma^{0.984\pm0.002}$ at $\approx4.7\gamma$ (the cap $\approx L - 1$ of the main text); a crossover around $\gamma\approx0.1$, where $\gamma L\approx h$, with a maximum $5.7\gamma$; strong dephasing, $\gamma\gtrsim1$, with $\Gamma\to2\gamma$. The non-scar rates are also linear in $\gamma$ for weak dephasing ($\gamma^{0.977}$ for P2, $\gamma^{1.001}$ for P3). At strong dephasing the energy probe slows down ($1.27\gamma$ at $\gamma = 6$).
