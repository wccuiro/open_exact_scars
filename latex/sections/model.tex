\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{Hamiltonian.} Spin-$1/2$ ring of $L$ sites (periodic boundaries), $S^\alpha_j = \sigma^\alpha_j/2$, bond singlet projector $R_{j,j+1} = \tfrac14 - \mathbf S_j\cdot\mathbf S_{j+1}$:
\begin{equation}
H = H_I + h S^z_{\mathrm{tot}}, \qquad
H_I = \sum_j \Big[ \sum_\alpha \big( a_\alpha R_{j,j+1}\sigma^\alpha_{j+2} + b_\alpha \sigma^\alpha_{j-1}R_{j,j+1} \big)
+ \sum_{\alpha\beta} c^{(j)}_{\alpha\beta}\, \sigma^\beta_{j-1} R_{j,j+1} \sigma^\alpha_{j+2} \Big].
\end{equation}
Every term of $H_I$ annihilates the ferromagnetic multiplet $\ket{\Phi_n} \propto (S^-_{\mathrm{tot}})^n\ket{\uparrow\cdots\uparrow}$, $n = 0,\dots,L$, so $H\ket{\Phi_n} = h(L/2-n)\ket{\Phi_n}$: an equally spaced tower of $L+1$ exact scars.

\paragraph{Coefficients.} $h = 1$, $a = b = (1, 1, 1)$, and $c_{xx} = c_{yy} = c_{zz} = 1$ (all other $c_{\alpha\beta} = 0$), each of the three multiplied on every bond $j$ by an independent factor uniform in $[0.5, 1.5]$. The random $c$ removes translation and reflection symmetry; $a$ and $b$ break SU(2), U(1), spin inversion and complex conjugation. One realization per $L$ (seed $1000 + L$), shared by every run; three more realizations (seeds 2, 3, 4) are used as a check in Sec.~\ref{sec:states}. Time is measured in units of $1/h$.

\paragraph{The scars among the eigenstates.} Figure~\ref{fig:ent} shows the half-chain entanglement entropy $S_A$ of all $2^L$ eigenstates of $H$ against their energy (dense diagonalization, cut at $L_A = \lfloor L/2\rfloor$). Exactly $L + 1$ eigenstates lie in the tower (tower weight $\sum_n\lvert\langle\Phi_n\vert E\rangle\rvert^2 = 1$ to $10^{-6}$), at $E = h(L/2 - n)$; their entropies are those of the Dicke states to $5\times10^{-13}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory-scars}). The bulk forms a thermal dome: in the middle of the spectrum it approaches the Page value $S_{\mathrm{Page}} = L_A\ln2 - 2^{2L_A - L - 1}$ of a random state (mean $S_A$ of the bulk states with $\lvert E\rvert < 1$: $0.90$, $0.92$, $0.96$, $0.97$ of it for $L = 6, 8, 10, 12$). The scars are the low-entanglement outliers: the two edge scars are product states ($S_A = 0$) and the middle one has $S_A = 1.32$ at $L = 12$, while the least entangled bulk state with $\lvert E\rvert < 1$ has $S_A = 3.41$. Low-entanglement bulk states exist only at the edges of the spectrum.

\subsection{Dissipation}
\label{sec:dissipation}

The system is coupled to a Markovian bath that acts on every site independently and in the same way. The density matrix obeys the Lindblad equation
\begin{equation}
\dot\rho = \Lv(\rho) = -i[H,\rho] + \sum_{j}\sum_{k}\Big(L_{j,k}\,\rho\,L_{j,k}^\dagger - \tfrac12\big\{L_{j,k}^\dagger L_{j,k},\, \rho\big\}\Big),
\label{eq:lindblad}
\end{equation}
with one or two jump operators $L_{j,k}$ per site $j$, all proportional to $\sqrt\gamma$. Six channels are used (Table~\ref{tab:channels}); $\sigma^z$ dephasing is the main one, and every result is for it unless stated otherwise.

$\sigma^x$, $\sigma^y$ dephasing & $\sqrt\gamma\,\sigma^x_j$, $\sqrt\gamma\,\sigma^y_j$ & yes & $\mathds 1/2$ \\

\paragraph{Physical meaning.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\sigma^z$ dephasing}: a fluctuating random field along $z$ (white noise, the $T_2$ process of a qubit). It leaves the populations of $\ket\uparrow$, $\ket\downarrow$ alone and destroys the coherences between them at rate $2\gamma$. Equivalently, with rate $\gamma$ per site the spin is rotated by $\pi$ about $z$ (a ``kick'' $\sigma^z_j$), at random times: the jump picture used in Secs.~\ref{sec:theory} and~\ref{sec:kick}.
\item \textbf{$\sigma^x$, $\sigma^y$ dephasing}: the same with a random field along $x$ or $y$, i.e.\ random $\pi$ rotations about $x$ or $y$. They damp the two spin components perpendicular to their axis and conserve the one along it.
\item \textbf{Depolarizing}: random rotations about all three axes, each at rate $\gamma/2$. Isotropic: every spin component decays at $2\gamma$, towards the fully mixed spin.
\item \textbf{Decay}: spontaneous emission into a zero-temperature bath ($T_1$ process); the spin relaxes to $\ket\downarrow$. Not unital: $\mathds 1/2^L$ is not a steady state.
\item \textbf{Thermal}: emission and absorption with a bath of mean occupation $\bar n$, obeying detailed balance; the free spin relaxes to the thermal state with $\langle\sigma^z\rangle = -1/(2\bar n+1)$ ($-1/3$ for $\bar n = 1$). $\bar n = 0$ is the decay channel; $\bar n \to \infty$ at fixed $\gamma\bar n$ makes it unital.
\end{itemize}
None of these jump operators lies in the bond algebra of the scars: they all move weight out of the tower. Jump operators taken from that algebra (e.g.\ $R_{j,j+1}\sigma^x_{j+2}$) would annihilate the tower and make it decoherence-free; they are not studied here.

\paragraph{Limits.} At $\gamma = 0$ the dynamics is unitary and the tower states revive perfectly (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory-scars}). At small $\gamma$ the noise is a weak perturbation of $H$: at fixed $L$ every rate is $\propto\gamma$, but the limits $\gamma \to 0$ and $L \to \infty$ need not commute (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory-weak}). At large $\gamma$, $\gamma \gg h$ and $\gamma \gg$ the couplings of $H_I$, every spin decays on its own: a component that the channel damps decays at the free-spin rate, while a component that the channel conserves is frozen by the quantum Zeno effect and evolves only at a rate $\propto h^2/\gamma$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory-channels}). For $\sigma^z$ dephasing the steady states are $\mathds 1/2^L$ and the two dark states $\dyad{\Phi_0}{\Phi_0}$, $\dyad{\Phi_L}{\Phi_L}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:exact}); for decay the unique steady state is $\dyad{\Phi_L}{\Phi_L} = \dyad{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}$, the bottom of the tower.

\paragraph{Methods and their limits.} All results come from five kinds of calculation, described in Appendix~\ref{sec:methods}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{exact evolution of $\rho$} in the basis of Pauli strings ($4^L$ real coordinates), every channel, $L \le 14$ ($14.7$ GB and $\approx 1$ hour per unit of time on a 112-core node at $L = 14$; in practice $L \le 12$ for the channel study);
\item \textbf{quantum trajectories}, pure states of $2^L$ amplitudes, diffusive (every Hermitian channel) or jump unravelling ($\sigma^z$ only), $L \le 22$; the cost per trajectory doubles with every site and the statistical error shrinks only as one over the square root of the number of trajectories; decay and thermal noise have no trajectory code;
\item \textbf{single-kick runs}: one pure state evolved with $H$ after one $\sigma^z$ kick, $L \le 24$;
\item \textbf{dense diagonalization} of $H$ ($L \le 12$) and of $\Lv$ ($L \le 7$, a dense $4^L\times4^L$ matrix);
\item \textbf{tensor networks} for larger systems: $\rho$ as a matrix product state evolved with TDVP, Lindbladian eigenvalues from time-evolved states (CLIK), and forward/backward evolution (Appendix~\ref{app:other}). The noisy $\rho$ from the scar state needs nearly the full bond dimension (the part kicked out of the tower thermalizes and carries large operator entanglement), and a single kicked pure state already reaches the maximal bond dimension of $L = 20$ by $t \approx 2.5$. They have not reached any size beyond the exact methods.
\end{itemize}
Every rate is the $1/e$ rate of the envelope of a signal, extrapolated in $1/L$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:rates}).
